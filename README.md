# Havells India: Three-Statement Model & DCF Valuation

A fully linked operating model and discounted cash flow valuation of **Havells India Limited** (NSE: HAVELLS), built from the company's filed FY2026 results.

> **Status:** the model is built, balances, and is fully analysed; see [Conclusion](#conclusion) for the finding and what's still an open item.

---

## What this is

A three-statement model is an income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement wired together by formulas, so that changing a single assumption ripples correctly through all three and the balance sheet still balances. On top of that sits a discounted cash flow valuation, which forecasts the cash the business will produce, converts those future rupees into today's rupees, and sums them to an intrinsic value per share.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Company** | Havells India Limited (NSE: HAVELLS, BSE: 517354) |
| **Basis** | Standalone (subsidiaries are ~0.3% of revenue) |
| **Historicals** | FY2025A – FY2026A |
| **Forecast** | FY2027E – FY2031E |
| **Units** | INR crore |
| **Valuation method** | DCF on free cash flow to firm, Gordon growth terminal value, mid-year discounting |
| **Cross-checks** | Sensitivity grid, reverse DCF, trading comparables |

**This repo also contains two companion models**, both built on top of this operating forecast:

- **`lbo/`** models a hypothetical leveraged buyout of Havells, explicitly framed as a mechanics exercise, not a deal thesis, since Havells' promoter control and scale make it an unrealistic real-world LBO target.
- **`merger/`** flips the direction: Havells as the acquirer of a real, smaller company (IFB Industries), a genuinely plausible bolt-on given Havells' balance sheet strength and its Lloyd segment's disclosed competitive weakness. The model finds the illustrative deal is meaningfully EPS-dilutive in Year 1, and quantifies exactly what it would take to break even.

See each folder's own README for the full explanation and the models themselves.

The workbook is generated by code rather than hand-built. Run `python scripts/build_model.py` to rebuild it from source at any time. This also means the model's structure is version-controlled and reviewable, which a raw `.xlsx` in git is not.

## Four things that make Havells specific

**1. It has no borrowings.** The only debt-like liability on the balance sheet is Ind AS 116 lease liabilities of ₹265 crore. Net debt is *negative*: the company holds ₹2,351 crore of cash and bank balances. Consequences: WACC is effectively pure cost of equity, and the equity bridge *adds* net cash rather than subtracting net debt. The model does not fabricate a capital structure to make the WACC formula look interesting.

**2. The Goldi Solar fair value gain is excluded from EBIT.** Havells invested ₹600 crore in Goldi Solar in Q1 FY26 and recognised a ₹283 crore unrealised fair value gain in Q4 FY26. This is a paper mark-up on a minority stake in a business Havells does not operate. Including it in operating earnings would inflate FCFF in every forecast year. The model excludes it from EBIT and instead carries the stake into the equity bridge at its ₹883 crore carrying value. The ₹45 crore exceptional item is treated the same way.

**3. Revenue is forecast segment by segment.** Cables (39% of revenue, +20.8% in FY26), Lloyd Consumer (18%, −22.9%), ECD (17%), Switchgears (11.5%), Others/solar (7.7%), Lighting (7.4%). A single blended growth rate would hide the fact that FY26's flat headline masked a strong cables year and a collapsing air-conditioner year.

**4. The P&L format follows the company's own.** Havells reports *contribution* (net revenue less material cost, manufacturing variables, direct selling variables and depreciation) rather than gross profit. The model replicates that, so every line ties directly to the filed investor update.

## Verification

Run after any change:

```bash
python scripts/build_model.py
python scripts/recalc_check.py            # or open in Excel and check manually
```

The model is only usable if **`BS!D29:H29` reads zero in every column.** A non-zero check means the balance sheet does not balance and the output is meaningless. (`BS!B29` reads 1, which is the company's own rounding in its abridged filed balance sheet, not a model error.)

## Conclusion

All three items below are done. What follows is what was actually found, not a to-do list.

- [x] **`docs/assumptions_log.md`**: WACC inputs are sourced and dated (Section 4); revenue/margin drivers remain the analyst's own judgment calls by design, with the evidence and "what would break it" for each documented.
- [x] **`WACC` tab**: risk-free rate 7.02% (India 10-year G-Sec, 11-Sep-2026), beta 0.90 (published third-party estimate; a self-run regression via `scripts/beta_regression.py` against a live price series is still a disclosed gap, not silently assumed away), India equity risk premium 7.31% (Damodaran, July 2026). Cost of equity 13.60%, WACC 13.57%.
- [x] **`docs/investment_memo.md`**: the two-page written conclusion, built around a reverse DCF.

**The finding:** at conservative but defensible assumptions, intrinsic value comes out to roughly Rs 258/share against a Rs 1,117 market price. Rather than stopping at "the stock looks 77% overvalued" (the least useful sentence a DCF can produce), the model solves numerically for what the market must be assuming instead. Holding WACC and the five-year explicit forecast fixed, the price implies a **perpetual terminal growth rate of roughly 12.1%**, which exceeds any reasonable estimate of India's long-run nominal GDP growth and is mathematically impossible to sustain forever. The (WACC − g) spread at that growth rate is only 1.4 percentage points, meaning the valuation would also be sitting in a highly unstable part of the Gordon growth formula. The conclusion isn't that the market is wrong; it's that the market is very unlikely to be pricing Havells off a standard perpetuity-growth DCF at all, and the full reasoning for that is in `docs/investment_memo.md`.

**Still genuinely open**, not hidden: the beta is a sourced third-party figure, not a self-run regression, and a peer comps tab to cross-check the implied terminal multiple hasn't been built. Both are flagged explicitly in `docs/assumptions_log.md`.
